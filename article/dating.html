Dating with Purpose: Navigating Relationships God's Way

How do you date as a Christian on a secular campus? Explore biblical principles for relationships that honor God and each other.

"I remember sitting in the campus cafeteria, watching couples walk by hand in hand. I felt a mix of longing and confusion. I wanted what they had—but I also wanted to honor God. I wasn't sure those two things could coexist."

That's how Grace, a third-year student, described her first year at university. She had grown up in church, knew all the right answers, but when it came to dating, everything felt murky. The pressure to pair up was real. The temptation to compromise was subtle. And the fear of being alone? That was loud.

Maybe you've felt that too. Maybe you're wondering: Can I date as a Christian and still have fun? Is it possible to honor God in a relationship without being weird or legalistic? How do I know if someone is "the one"?

Let's talk about it—honestly, biblically, and practically.

Dating Is Not a Sin—But It's Not a Game Either

First things first: dating is not wrong. God designed us for relationship, and the desire for connection, intimacy, and partnership is good. The Bible celebrates love and marriage (see Song of Solomon, Proverbs 18:22, Hebrews 13:4).

But dating as a Christian means something different from what the world often models. It's not about playing games, using people, or seeing how far you can go without "technically" crossing a line. It's about honoring God and honoring the person in front of you—whether or not you end up together.

"So whether you eat or drink or whatever you do, do it all for the glory of God."

1 Corinthians 10:31 (NIV)

That verse applies to dating too. Every conversation, every hangout, every physical boundary—it all matters to God. Not because He's a cosmic killjoy, but because He knows what leads to life and what leads to heartbreak.

Dating isn't about finding someone to complete you. It's about sharing your life with someone who points you toward Christ.

The Foundation: Be Equally Yoked

One of the most important principles in Scripture for relationships is found in 2 Corinthians:

"Do not be yoked together with unbelievers. For what do righteousness and wickedness have in common? Or what fellowship can light have with darkness?"

2 Corinthians 6:14 (NIV)

This doesn't mean you can't be friends with non-Christians—you absolutely should be. But when it comes to romantic relationships, the Bible is clear: shared faith is non-negotiable. Why? Because the person you marry will shape your spiritual life more than anyone else. If they don't share your love for Christ, you'll be pulling in different directions.

Ask yourself: Does this person love Jesus more than they love me? If the answer is no, that's a red flag. A godly partner will encourage your faith, not compete with it.

Purity Is Not About Rules—It's About Trust

Let's be honest: the word "purity" has often been used to shame people, especially women. That's not God's heart. Purity isn't about following a list of rules or earning God's love. It's about living in a way that honors God and protects your heart, your body, and your future.

"It is God's will that you should be sanctified: that you should avoid sexual immorality; that each of you should learn to control your own body in a way that is holy and honorable."

1 Thessalonians 4:3-4 (NIV)

God's design for intimacy is within marriage—not because He wants to limit your fun, but because He knows that sex is a soul-deep connection that carries weight. Outside of covenant, it often leaves wounds.

Practically, this means setting boundaries before you're in the moment. Decide ahead of time what you will and won't do. Be honest with each other. And if you've already crossed lines, know this: God's grace is bigger than your past. Repentance brings forgiveness, and healing is possible.

Purity is not about being perfect. It's about being honest, repentant, and moving forward in grace.

Love Is Not a Feeling—It's a Choice

Our culture tells us love is a feeling that comes and goes. The Bible defines love differently:

"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It does not dishonor others, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres."

1 Corinthians 13:4-7 (NIV)

Read that again—slowly. Now ask yourself: Does this describe how I treat the person I'm interested in? Does it describe how they treat me?

Love is not just butterflies and late-night texts. It's patience when they're late. Kindness when you're tired. Choosing to protect their reputation, their purity, and their walk with God—even when it costs you something.

Practical Steps for Dating with Purpose

1. Get Healthy Before You Get Together

You can't have a healthy relationship if you're not healthy yourself. Work on your relationship with God, your emotional wounds, and your community first. A person who is desperate for love will make decisions a person who is secure in Christ wouldn't.

2. Date with Intentionality

Know what you're looking for. If you're dating just for fun, be honest about that. But if you're dating with the possibility of marriage in mind, be clear. Don't lead someone on, and don't let yourself be led on.

3. Involve Wise People

Don't date in isolation. Invite mentors, pastors, and trusted friends into your relationship. They will see things you can't see. Proverbs 15:22 says, "Plans fail for lack of counsel, but with many advisers they succeed."

4. Guard Your Heart—But Don't Build a Fortress

Proverbs 4:23 says, "Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it." Guarding your heart doesn't mean being emotionally closed off. It means being wise, taking things at a healthy pace, and not giving away pieces of yourself to someone who hasn't earned that trust.

5. Put God First—Always

No human relationship can take the place of God. If you're looking to a person to fill the void that only God can fill, you'll be disappointed every time. Keep God at the center, and everything else will fall into place.

"But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well."

Matthew 6:33 (NIV)

If You're Single Right Now, You're Not Behind

One of the biggest lies on campus is that you're incomplete until you're in a relationship. That's not true. Singleness is not a waiting room—it's a season with purpose. Paul actually encouraged singleness in 1 Corinthians 7 because it allows for undivided devotion to God.

Use this season to grow. Serve. Build friendships. Discover your calling. Become the kind of person you want to marry. And trust that God's timing is better than your own.

Dating with purpose isn't about being perfect. It's about being intentional. It's about asking God to shape your desires, your boundaries, and your heart. And it's about trusting that whether you're single, dating, or waiting, God is writing a story bigger than you can see.

So take a breath. Be honest with God about what you want. And remember: you are not defined by your relationship status. You are defined by who you are in Christ.
