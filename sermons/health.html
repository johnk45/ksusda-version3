Health and Spiritual Wellness

Biblical Principles on Diet, Appetite, and Physical Health

  • Overview
  • Appetite Control
  • Mind & Body
  • Daniel's Example
  • Key Principles

Biblical Foundation

The Bible presents a profound connection between physical health and spiritual wellness. During the antitypical day of atonement, when individuals are being accounted worthy or unworthy of eternal life, we are called to exercise mastery over our appetites and desires.

"Take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, and drunkenness, and cares of this life, and so that day come upon you unawares…. Watch ye therefore, and pray always that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man."

Luke 21:34-36

The prophet Isaiah provides a vivid description of the contrast between those who heed God's call and those who ignore it:

"In that day did the Lord God of hosts call to weeping, and to mourning, and to baldness, and to girding with sackcloth: and behold, joy and gladness, slaying oxen, and killing sheep, eating flesh and drinking wine: let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die."

Isaiah 22:12-13

Mastery Over Appetite

God calls His people to be masters of their appetites, not slaves to them. This distinction is crucial for spiritual development and maintaining a clear mind for discerning divine truth.

What is Surfeiting?

Surfeiting is taking too freely of food, whether it be good or bad. It refers to overindulgence and excessive consumption, even of wholesome foods.

What is Drunkenness?

Drunkenness in this context refers to partaking of improper foods—those that are injurious to health and cloud the mind. The glutton and drunkard are classified together in Scripture:

"Be not among winebibbers; among riotous eaters of flesh; for the drunkard and the glutton shall come to poverty."

Proverbs 23:20-21
Key Point: One can becloud the mind and ruin the health by overeating of even the best of food. Both quantity and quality of food matter.

The Rule for Eating

Scripture provides practical guidance on consuming even good foods:

  • "My son, eat thou honey, because it is good." - Proverbs 24:13
  • "Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee." - Proverbs 25:16
  • "It is not good to eat much honey." - Proverbs 25:27

These three texts establish a rule for eating: consume only good foods, and only so much as is sufficient. Those who follow this principle will enjoy good health and a clear mind.

"Our first parents failed on the test of appetite, but where they failed, Christ triumphed. It is possible for a Christian, with the indwelling Christ in the heart, to be complete master of his appetite—to abstain from all food that is injurious, however much the natural appetite may crave it."

The Mind-Body Connection

Understanding how appetite affects our mental and spiritual capacity is essential for spiritual wellness.

Impact on the Nervous System

The brain nerves that communicate with the entire system are the only medium through which Heaven can communicate to us and affect our inmost life. Whatever disturbs the circulation of the electric currents in the nervous system lessens the strength of vital powers and deadens the sensibilities of the mind.

Spiritual Consequences

When the brain is clouded by improper diet, spiritual perception is dimmed. God desires His people to have clear minds to comprehend divine truth and follow His work in the heavenly sanctuary.

The Role of Flesh Meat

The use of flesh meat strengthens the lower propensities and excites them to increased activity. Parents who allow their children to eat meat freely, use butter and eggs excessively, will likely see:

  • Disease in various forms
  • Impaired physical health
  • Weakened mental capacity
  • Diminished spirituality
  • Increased excitation of base passions
Important: Man is built up from what he eats. Parents should discard everything that endangers the moral and physical health of their children.

The Daniel Fast: A Model for Health

The prophet Daniel provides an excellent example of using dietary discipline to achieve spiritual clarity and understanding:

"In those days I Daniel was mourning three full weeks. I ate no pleasant bread, neither came flesh nor wine in my mouth, neither did I anoint myself at all, till three whole weeks were fulfilled."

Daniel 10:2-3

Understanding Daniel's Approach

When Daniel wanted to understand a vision, he engaged in what we now call a "Daniel Fast." But importantly, he did not stop eating entirely. Instead, he ate clean, wholesome foods to maintain a clear brain for receiving divine understanding.

The Daniel Fast Today

Today, God calls His people to a similar practice—not a complete fast from eating, but a conscious choice to eat only foods that promote mental clarity and spiritual receptivity. This allows us to:

  • Maintain physical strength
  • Achieve mental clarity
  • Enhance spiritual perception
  • Prepare for eternal life

"In the third year of Cyrus king of Persia a thing was revealed unto Daniel, whose name was called Belteshazzar; and the thing was true, but the time appointed was long: and he understood the thing, and had understanding of the vision."

Daniel 10:1

Essential Health Principles

Christ's Example

Jesus Christ was tested on appetite from childhood. The prophet Isaiah says:

"Butter and honey shall He eat, that He may know to refuse the evil, and choose the good."

Isaiah 7:15

Jesus ate according to Biblical rules, which developed in Him spiritual strength and power to discern between good and evil. His manner of eating became a model for all believers.

God's Purpose for Our Health

"Beloved, I wish above all things that thou mayest prosper and be in health, even as thy soul prospereth."

3 John 2

God desires His people to have good health with souls free from condemnation. Satan, on the other hand, delights in beclouding the brain and destroying health.

Testing Times and Divine Care

Those who practice simple diet and develop fixed habits of wholesome eating during their probation will be sustained even in times of drought:

"Their bread and water will be sure, and habits of simple diet have become so fixed during their time of probation that though there may be a drought, God can feed them as He did Israel in the wilderness."

Comparison: Mastery vs. Slavery to Appetite

Mastery Over Appetite Slavery to Appetite
Clear, sharp mind Clouded, sluggish thinking
Spiritual perception heightened Spiritual blindness
Good physical health Various diseases and ailments
Control of base passions Excitement of base appetites
Prepared for eternity Unfit for God's kingdom
Blessed with divine wisdom Deceived by seducing spirits

Solemn Warnings

"Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils."

1 Timothy 4:1-2

In these last days, many will be deceived about what foods are permissible. However, Scripture assures us:

"For every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving: for it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer."

1 Timothy 4:4-5
  • Those who practice intemperance will be "consumed together" says the Lord
  • The indulgence of appetite clouds the mind and separates us from God
  • Intemperance in eating is as serious a sin as drunkenness
  • Only through Christ's indwelling power can we achieve complete mastery
"Satan delights in beclouding the brain and destroying the health. All who will fulfil the antitype will be masters of their appetites, that they may prepare to meet the Saviour when He comes to the earth as King of kings and Lord of lords."

Conclusion

A Call to Holistic Health

The biblical message on health is clear: we are called to be masters of our appetites, not slaves to them. This mastery isn't about legalism or deprivation—it's about freedom. When we control what we eat and how much we eat, we free our minds to commune with God, comprehend spiritual truths, and prepare ourselves for eternity.

By following the examples of Daniel and Christ, by understanding the connection between our physical habits and spiritual capacity, and by trusting in God's power working through us, we can achieve the wholeness—physical, mental, and spiritual—that God desires for His people.
